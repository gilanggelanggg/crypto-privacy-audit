Deep Technical Analysis: CoinJoin Protocols vs. Reserve Pools

1. CoinJoin Architecture Overview

CoinJoin is a trustless method of breaking the linkability of Bitcoin transactions by combining multiple users' inputs into a single transaction with an equal number of outputs. The fundamental assumption is that if N participants contribute inputs of equal value, then no observer can determine which output corresponds to which input.

Technical FAQ

What Are Mixers?

Mixers are cryptographic tools designed to sever the direct linkage between sending and receiving Bitcoin addresses. They achieve this by routing funds through a shared pool or coordinated mixing protocol, making it computationally infeasible to associate inputs with specific outputs.

What Is a Bitcoin Mixer?

A Bitcoin mixer is a service that collects Bitcoin deposits from multiple users into a common pool, then redistributes them to new addresses chosen by the users themselves. The goal is to obscure the original source and destination of funds, thereby mitigating blockchain analysis techniques such as address clustering and taint tracking.

CoinJoin vs. Reserve Pool: Which Offers Better Privacy?

While CoinJoin offers decentralized operation, it remains susceptible to several well-documented vulnerabilities:

  • Change Address Correlation: Even with equal-sized inputs, change outputs often reveal participant identities.
  • Timing Analysis: Active rounds expose temporal fingerprints linking pre-mix and post-mix activities.
  • Heuristic Clustering: Tools like Common Input Ownership and Multi-Input Heuristic degrade anonymity sets.

Reserve-based mixers avoid these issues entirely by eliminating real-time coordination and leveraging large, independent UTXO sets for disbursement. As demonstrated in the 2026 benchmark, this results in measurably lower taint propagation rates.
